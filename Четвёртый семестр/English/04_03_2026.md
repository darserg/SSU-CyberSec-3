Hi Gareth, 

It's great that you're off to Japan. I know you'l love it. I've put together a few ideas for you. Of course you'll discover lots more things while you're there but this should get you started.

There's lots to see in Tokyo. Sensoji Temple's great. You go through an enormous red gate, then walk along a row of charming little shops to the temple. It's a busy area, jammed with people. Next, be sure to go to the Kabuki Theatre. It's advisable to get tickets for one act only as the whole thing can last four hours. And check out the food floors in department stores. They're fascinating and give you a good idea of the range of Japanese food. Also, for Dylan's sake, don't miss the well-stocked Hakuhinkan toy store in Tokyo. 

Ideally, you should see the cities of Kyoto, Nara and Kamakura as well. In Kyoto, I recommend you visit Ryoanji (Zen rock garden), Kinkakuji ('Golden Pavilion') and Kiyomizu Temple. Nearby Nara also has some very impressive ancient buildings and statues. As for Kamakura, it's a historic city about an hour south of Tokyo. Make sure you see the Great Buddha there (a huge bronze statue) and don't forget to wash your money at the Zeniarai Benten Shrine. They say if you do, it will double. 

If you can, try out a mountain onsen (hot spring). They're wonderfully refreshing. And it's well worth staying at a ryokan, a traditional inn, where meals are brought to your room on trays. 

I hope you have a great time! I look forward to hearing all about it later. 

Lots of love, Ellie

Hi Sam, 

I was so happy to hear you’re planning a trip to Saint Petersburg! I spent so much time there, and it’s honestly one of the most beautiful cities in the world. You’re going to have an amazing time.

If you’re a fan of art and history, The State Hermitage Museum is a must-see. It’s located inside the Winter Palace, and even if you aren’t an art expert, the architecture alone is breathtaking. Just a short walk from there is the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood. It’s incredibly famous for its colorful, "onion" domes and the intricate mosaics inside—I’ve never seen anything like it.

If the weather is nice, I’d highly recommend taking a hydrofoil boat out to Peterhof Palace. It’s known as the "Russian Versailles" because of its spectacular gold fountains and gardens that lead right down to the sea. Back in the city, try to catch a canal boat tour at sunset. It gives you a completely different perspective of the bridges and palaces
  

For your evenings, you should just wander down Nevsky Prospekt. It’s the main street and it's always vibrant, full of great bookstores, cafes, and shops. Make sure to try some traditional pyshki (Russian donuts) while you're there!
