People skydive for various reasons, such as seeking an adrenaline rush, overcoming fears, or experiencing a sense of freedom and accomplishment.

### **p20-ex2**
The correct matching of topics a–d with paragraphs 1–4 is:
- **Paragraph 1 → c (background to the story)** — It introduces Charlie’s fear of heights and how he ended up skydiving.
- **Paragraph 2 → d (first actions)** — It describes what happened immediately after his parachute failed and his attempt to fix it.
- **Paragraph 3 → a (accepting the situation)** — It shows Charlie realizing he was going to die and his emotional reaction during the fall.
- **Paragraph 4 → b (survival and afterwards)** — It covers his recovery, injuries, and reflections on being alive.

### **p20-ex3**
Charlie seems to be a brave, reflective, and resilient person who faced an extreme situation with surprising calmness.  
**If I were Charlie, I probably would not dive again, because surviving such a terrifying accident would likely make me too afraid to risk it again. However, I admire his courage and ability to reflect positively on his survival.**

### **p20-ex4**
In paragraph 3, Charlie uses these techniques to make his story exciting:
1. **A quotation which shows how Charlie thought and felt:** “Right, I’m dead.” — This direct thought reveals his acceptance of death in a dramatic, immediate way.
2. **A use of the past progressive to emphasise that an action happened over a period of time:** “All the time I was falling and turning, with no control.” — This highlights the prolonged, helpless nature of his fall.
3. **A series of short, dramatic sentences:** “I briefly saw the ground and a little shack. Then I hit the roof of the shack. I bounced off it, hit a wall and then the ground.” — These quick, punchy sentences create tension and vividly convey the speed and impact of the crash.

### **p20-ex5**
Useful expressions for storytelling:
1. **To introduce a story:** “I’ve always...” and “one day” are useful to set up background and begin the narrative.
2. **To finish a story:** “I still can’t believe...” and “since then” are effective for concluding with reflection or showing lasting impact.
