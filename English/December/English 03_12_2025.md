### **p25-ex1**

a. Classification of sentences 1–7:
- **About the present:**
  - **Sentence 2:** “From 10am to 4pm, he focuses on his own studies.” (Simple present — describes a current habit)
  - **Sentence 4:** “On a typical day, he’ll rise at 5am for morning prayers.” (will + infinitive — describes a future habitual action, often used for routines)
  - **Sentence 7:** “He’s always rushing from place to place.” (be always -ing — describes a present, irritating or frequent habit)

- **About the past:**
  - **Sentence 1:** “My friends helped me with the teaching.” (Simple past — describes a completed past action)
  - **Sentence 3:** “I would repeat everything for them.” (would + infinitive — describes a repeated past habit)
  - **Sentence 5:** “There used to be just eight pupils.” (used to + infinitive — describes a past state that is no longer true)
  - **Sentence 6:** “They were always asking questions about my lessons.” (be always -ing — describes a frequent past habit, often with a sense of annoyance or emphasis)

b. Matching forms A–D to their uses:
1. **To emphasise that something happens / happened very often?** → **D (be always -ing)** — This form is used to emphasise frequency, often with a tone of irritation or surprise, in both past and present.
2. **To describe habits or states in the past?** → **C (used to + infinitive)** — Used specifically for past habits or states that are no longer true.
3. **To describe habits or typical behaviour, but not states?** → **B (will/would + infinitive)** — “Will” for present/future habits, “would” for past habits; both describe actions or behaviors, not static conditions.
4. **To describe different things in the present and past: actions, habits, states, etc.?** → **A (Simple forms)** — The simple past and simple present can describe actions, habits, and states in either time frame.
