## :LiBookOpenText: Theory
### Present perfect Simple
1. Describing a period from some time in the past until now. You can use the present perfect simple in this way to summarise experiences and achievements
 **I've been** to Malaysia three times *(in my life)*
 **I've only** seen Julia since January
   
2. Describing an activity, state or habit which started in the past and is still going on. You often use the present perfect simple in this way to answer the question 'How long..?'
**I've lived** in the same place since 1960.
**They've used** the same doctor for twenty years.
3. Emphasising the present result of past events
I think my battery **has run out** *(= now my mobile isn't working)*
Darya's talent for numbers **has made** her a lot of money *(= now she's quite rich
)*

Because the present perfect simple is about the present, you can't use it with finished past time expressions
**I've been** to Malaysia ~~in 2007~~
### Present perfect progressive
1. Describing an activity, state or habit which started int the past and is still happening. This is similar to the the second use of present perfect above, so you can say:
**I've lived** in the same place since 1960.
`OR` **I've been living** in the same place since 1960.
**They've used** the same doctor for twenty years.
`OR` **They've been using** the same doctor for twenty years.
In situations like this, native speakers most often choose the progressive
2. Describing a past activity which has a direct result now. The result comes from the activity, and it doesn't matter if the activity finished or not.
